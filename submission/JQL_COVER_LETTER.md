# Journal of Quantitative Linguistics Cover Letter Draft

Date: 2026-06-09

Dear Editors,

I am submitting "Measuring Stable Diagnostic Ambiguity: A Quantitative Workflow for Small-*n* Grammatical Boundary Phenomena" for consideration as an article in the *Journal of Quantitative Linguistics*.

The paper develops a quantitative workflow for grammatical boundary phenomena where the target inventory is too small for ordinary large-sample classification. The case study is English reciprocals (*each other*, *one another*), which have long been treated as pronouns but show systematic affinities with compound determinatives. The article asks whether their mixed diagnostic profile is stable across measurement choices rather than selecting a single diagnostic to force a category decision.

The contribution is methodological as well as descriptive. The paper combines a 155-property grammatical feature profile with distance measurement, constrained randomization, specification-curve analysis, comparator-set robustness checks, predictive blending, and calibration against clear anchors. The result is a workflow for distinguishing unstable diagnostic conflict from stable boundary placement. I argue that such stability is theoretically informative for linguistic categorization: when categories are projectible property clusters, persistent ambiguity can itself be a measurable object of explanation.

The submission package includes an anonymous manuscript for double-anonymous review, a separate title page/declarations file, and supplementary data, code, generated figures, and analysis summaries.

The manuscript has been posted as a LingBuzz preprint. It is not under consideration elsewhere. An earlier submission to the *Canadian Journal of Linguistics/Revue canadienne de linguistique* was rejected without external review because the editors were unable to secure reviewers despite several attempts and suggested that the paper would fit better in a more specialized journal. No reviewer reports were received. I have retargeted the paper to JQL because its central contribution is the quantitative treatment of grammatical boundary phenomena.

Sincerely,

Brett Reynolds
