# JQL Framing Consistency Pass

Objective: make the manuscript consistently read as a quantitative-linguistics measurement paper, with property-cluster theory treated as a light interpretive payoff rather than the central framework.

## Editing Direction

1. Keep the paper's main contribution as the workflow: feature-space measurement, constrained benchmarks, specification sensitivity, comparator rotation, predictive blending, and anchor calibration.
2. Avoid foregrounding `HPC` as a named framework in the abstract, introduction, discussion, or conclusion.
3. Retain the useful idea that grammatical categories can be projectible property clusters, but express it in ordinary terms unless the citation to Miller is doing necessary work.
4. Replace local “HPC lens/view/reading” phrasing with measurement-language alternatives:
   - stable interface placement
   - projectible property-cluster view
   - cross-dimensional tension
   - diagnostic uncertainty localized to boundary items
5. Remove mechanism-heavy language unless it is explicitly framed as an interpretation the measurements do not establish.
6. Keep the conclusion JQL-facing: the payoff is a reproducible quantitative workflow for small-`n` grammatical category edges.

## Likely Edits

- Abstract: keep “projectible property clusters” if useful; avoid “homeostatic property-cluster interpretation.”
- Introduction: define the theoretical payoff without making `HPC` an acronym the reader must track.
- Discussion: replace “On an HPC view” and “Read through the HPC lens” with measurement-first language.
- Limitations/implications/conclusion: keep only cautious, non-central property-cluster phrasing.

## Non-Goals

- Do not change the empirical claims.
- Do not alter figures, tables, analysis scripts, or reported numbers.
- Do not expand the theory section to make HPC a full framework for JQL.
